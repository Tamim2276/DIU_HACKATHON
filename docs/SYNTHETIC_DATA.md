# Synthetic data and its assumptions

All data in this project is generated by a program. It describes no real person, and no real customer data was used to build or tune it. This file lists every assumption the generator makes, so anyone can judge how far the results can be trusted.

- **Code:** `backend/app/infrastructure/synthetic/` (`personas.py`, `generator.py`)
- **Run:** `python -m scripts.generate_data` from the `backend` folder
- **Output:** `backend/data/`
- **Settings:** `backend/app/infrastructure/config/settings.py`

## What is generated

| File | Contents | Seen by the model? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `users.csv` | User id, persona and persona label. | The id only. |
| `transactions.csv.gz` | One row per transaction: id, user, time, type, direction, amount, fee, counterparty, category, channel, balance after. | Yes. |
| `truth_daily.csv.gz` | Per user and day: end-of-day wallet balance, cash in hand, desired spending, unmet need, whether the user ran short, amount borrowed, payments waiting, payments missed. | No. Used only to grade the model. |
| `user_truth.csv` | Each user's hidden settings and their regular payments. | No. Used only in tests. |

The data covers 300 users (60 per persona) from 1 October 2025 to 30 September 2026. The random seed is 42. The same settings always produce byte-identical files.

## The five personas

Amounts are in taka and are rough guesses, not measured figures.

| Persona | How income arrives | Regular payments each month | Share of spending in cash |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ride-share rider | A payout of about 900 on a working day. Works on 87% of days. | House rent 5,000 (5th), money sent home 6,000 (8th), bike instalment 3,000 (15th) | 75% |
| Garment worker | One salary of about 16,500 around the 8th. Late by 2 to 6 days in 25% of months. | House rent 3,500 (10th), money sent home 5,000 (11th), savings group 1,000 (12th) | 80% |
| University student | An allowance of about 11,000 around the 3rd. Late in 30% of months. 60% of students also get about 4,000 from tutoring around the 12th. | Mess rent 3,500 (5th), internet 500 (7th) | 50% |
| Small shop owner | Sales of about 3,400 on a working day, as several customer payments. Open on 93% of days. | Shop rent 9,000 (5th), electricity 2,500 (12th), and a supplier payment every week equal to 72% of the last seven days' sales | 70% |
| Freelancer | On average 2.2 payments a month of about 15,000 each, on random days. The amount varies widely. | House rent 10,000 (5th), internet 1,200 (8th), electricity 1,500 (12th), subscriptions 1,500 (15th) | 40% |

## How each user differs

Every user gets their own hidden settings, drawn at random once:

- **Scale.** Income and regular payments are multiplied by a personal factor, so users of one persona differ in size.
- **Tightness.** Desired spending as a share of income: 93% to 107% for most personas, 90% to 103% for students, 80% to 97% for freelancers. Above 100% means the user wants to spend more than they earn.
- **Overspending after income.** People paid monthly or in lumps spend 10% to 90% more than usual right after income arrives. The effect fades over about four days. People paid daily do not have it.
- **Payment dates and amounts.** Each regular payment's day shifts by up to two days per user, and its amount by up to 15%.
- **Rent before income.** For a quarter of users paid monthly, the first regular payment falls due three to six days before the income usually arrives.
- **Cash-out habit.** Each user takes out two to five days of cash at a time.
- **Phone.** A persona-specific share of users use USSD instead of the app.

## What happens each day

For every user, every day, in this order:

1. **Income arrives** if it is due. A salary or allowance normally comes on its usual day or one day after. Daily earners have a 0.8% chance each day of starting a gap of three to six days with no income (illness, repairs, a strike).
2. **Earlier borrowing is repaid** once two days have passed and the wallet holds at least twice the borrowed amount.
3. **Due payments are made** before any daily spending. A payment the wallet cannot cover rolls to the next day. After seven days the user pays what they can and the rest is recorded as missed.
4. **Daily spending.** The user wants to spend a base amount, higher right after income, 15% higher on Fridays and Saturdays, 45% higher in the ten days before Eid, and with random day-to-day variation. Part is paid in cash and part digitally. Spending is limited by the money actually available.
5. **Cash.** When cash in hand cannot cover the day, the user cashes out a lump from an agent, rounded up to 500 taka and limited by the wallet balance.
6. **Borrowing.** After two short days in a row, a user with no outstanding borrowing receives three to six days of spending money from a contact.
7. **Saving.** A wallet holding more than 1.6 months of income sends the excess above one month to a bank.

A day counts as **short** when more than a quarter of that day's desired spending could not be met.

## Other assumptions

- **Cash-out fee:** 1.4% of the amount. This is an assumed rate held in one setting. It is not upay's official charge.
- **Other fees:** none. Sending money, paying bills and paying merchants are free in the simulation.
- **Eid:** assumed on 20 March and 27 May 2026. The real dates depend on the moon sighting. Shop sales are 50% higher in the ten days before each Eid. Garment workers receive a bonus of 60% of a month's salary eight days before each Eid.
- **Emergencies:** each day carries a 0.4% chance of an unplanned expense of 1,500 to 6,000 taka. Nobody, including the model, can predict these.
- **Mobile recharge:** 20, 30, 50 or 100 taka, on 15% to 35% of days depending on the user.
- **Merchant payments:** grocery 35%, food 30%, transport 15%, shopping 12%, pharmacy 8%.
- **Starting point:** each user starts with 15% to 45% of a month's income in the wallet and half a day to two days of spending in cash.
- **Labels on transfers:** the `category` of a regular transfer, such as `house_rent`, stands for a name the customer saved for that recipient. A real wallet provider would not know the purpose of a person-to-person transfer.

## Known limits

- The amounts and probabilities are guesses. They were chosen to look plausible, not fitted to real data.
- Real customers are more varied than five personas with equal numbers of users.
- Customers who withdraw their whole balance as cash on payday are not represented. Their wallet balance would say little about the money they have.
- The only seasonal effects are weekends and Eid.
- A model that works on this data has only been shown to recover patterns we put in. It still has to be validated on real, governed data.
